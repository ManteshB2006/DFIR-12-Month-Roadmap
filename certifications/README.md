# DFIR Certification Guidance

Certifications can structure learning and support an application, but they do not replace practical investigation skills or a portfolio. Check current exam objectives, prerequisites, prices, and availability before paying.

| Certification | Focus | When to consider |
|---|---|---|
| CompTIA Security+ | Broad security foundations | After basic networking and security knowledge |
| Blue Team Level 1 (BTL1) | Practical blue-team skills | After SOC and investigation fundamentals |
| CHFI | Computer-forensics concepts | If its syllabus and recognition fit your target roles |
| GIAC GCFE | Windows forensic analysis | Later, with stronger experience and budget |
| GIAC GCFA | Advanced incident response and forensics | Longer-term, after substantial practical experience |

## Recommended approach
1. Build fundamentals and complete free or low-cost labs first.
2. Choose one certification based on job descriptions you are targeting.
3. Compare its syllabus against skills you have already practised.
4. Budget for training, exam, retakes, and renewal where applicable.
5. Continue producing investigation reports while preparing.

Do not collect certifications at the expense of hands-on practice. A well-documented investigation that you can explain is valuable evidence of skill.
